# RoosterWorks Station Modules roadmap

## Included in v0.7.7

- Show or hide the mission flag on each module in the editor or in flight. Each part remembers its setting. One control operates both ends of the gravity ring.
- Use **Mirror wiring** to move the blue and yellow service lines and their clips to the opposite side of the handrail. Elbow wiring follows the bend, and the six-way hub switches all branches together. **Restore standard wiring** returns the original layout.

## Future work

- **Consistent exterior wiring.** Carry the blue and yellow lines to every mating face. Prototype two narrow, separate conductor rings recessed into a 2.5 m collar, with each wire landing on its own track. Try a straight stack, a flipped module, a rotated module and a hub side port. Check color continuity, handrail clearance and the appearance of an exposed end. If the rings look too busy, try a covered junction at the collar. Compare the prototypes in the VAB before extending one design to the adapters and the 3.75 m and 5 m families.
- **Purpose-built interior views (IVA).** Give crewed modules interiors that explain what the crew does there. A command seat should see out through the actual command windows. A habitat could have bunks and a fold-down table, a lab could have experiment racks, and an airlock could have suit restraints beside the EVA hatch. Prototype one room at crew eye height and check the seat count, window sightlines and hatch placement against the exterior.

## Ideas for consideration

- **Observation cupola.** A short panoramic module with one seat angled toward the planet and another looking along the station for visiting craft. Use the sightlines from inside to place the window frames.
- **EVA wayfinding.** Small port numbers on hub collars and matching markings inside at the hatches would let a player call out a destination such as “Port 3” and find it from either side. Try the markings on one six-way hub at EVA camera distance.
- **Crew lighting.** Try warm berth lamps in the habitat and a dim night setting in command. Check that the instrument faces stay readable and the planet stays visible through the windows on the dark side of an orbit.
